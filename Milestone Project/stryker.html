    Late 1990s
  • Weight:
    approx. 19–21 tons depending on variant
  • Speed:
    up to 60 mph (97 km/h)
  • Crew:
    2 crew + 9 infantry

History

The Stryker family was introduced in the early 2000s as a fast, air-transportable wheeled armored vehicle for rapid deployment forces. It was designed to fill the gap between heavy tracked vehicles and lighter HMMWVs.


Design and Features:


The Stryker ICV features 8×8 wheeled mobility, high-speed capability, and a fully enclosed armored hull. It includes a Remote Weapon Station (RWS), advanced communications, and optional armor upgrades like slat armor or double-V hulls for IED protection.


Operational History:


Strykers have been deployed in Iraq, Afghanistan, and other theaters, showing their value in urban and rapid-reaction combat scenarios. Their modularity and speed have made them crucial for infantry transport and reconnaissance.


Upgrades and Export:


Modern Stryker variants include the Dragoon (30mm cannon), MEHEL (laser systems), and others. While primarily used by the U.S. Army, some allied forces have adopted or evaluated the platform due to its versatility and speed.


Legacy:


The Stryker redefined U.S. Army doctrine by enabling rapid deployment and operational flexibility. It remains a central component of American brigade combat teams and continues to evolve to meet modern battlefield threats.
