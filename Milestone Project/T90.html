History

The T-90A is an upgraded variant of the original T-90, which itself is a continuation of the Soviet T-72 platform. First introduced in the early 2000s, the T-90A incorporated major improvements in armor, electronics, and firepower, positioning it as Russia's most capable tank until the introduction of the T-14 Armata.


Design and Features:


The T-90A features Kontakt-5 explosive reactive armor (ERA), a modernized turret with better ballistic protection, and a thermal imaging sight for the gunner. The 125mm smoothbore cannon can fire a variety of rounds including anti-tank guided missiles (ATGMs). Its low silhouette and compact design make it harder to target in open terrain.


Operational History:


The T-90A has seen combat in various conflicts including the Syrian Civil War and the Russo-Ukrainian War. It has demonstrated a high degree of survivability and continues to serve as a backbone of Russian armored forces. Its export variants have also been used by countries like India and Algeria.


Upgrades and Export:


Russia has exported T-90 variants such as the T-90S and T-90MS to several allied countries. The T-90A itself was upgraded from earlier models with a welded turret, improved thermal optics, and a more powerful engine. Export models often feature region-specific modifications based on client needs.


Legacy:


The T-90A remains in active service and has influenced the design of newer tanks in both Russia and abroad. It is widely regarded as a capable and cost-effective third-generation main battle tank, respected for its battlefield performance and modern systems.
